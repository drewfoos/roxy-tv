## Key Question 1: What formats do anime releases actually use, and how do the three servers classify them (direct play / remux / transcode)?

### Takeaway
Anime releases are overwhelmingly MKV with HEVC or H.264 video (10-bit for "high-quality anime encodes"), ASS subtitles with embedded font attachments, and FLAC/AAC/Opus/TrueHD/DTS-HD audio; AV1 is not yet a mainstream anime release codec per the main community quality guide. Servers direct-play when the client supports container+video+audio+subtitle; otherwise they remux (container swap, cheap) or transcode (expensive), and subtitle burn-in is explicitly the most CPU-intensive path in Jellyfin's own documentation.

### Cited Findings
- Anime release conventions (thewiki.moe, "release information is current as of 13th August 2026"): "HEVC (H.265) and AVC (H.264) are the main ones"; bit depth is "Typically in 8-bit, or 10-bit for high-quality anime encodes"; a 10-bit encode can be smaller at the same quality; lossless audio options are DTS-HD MA, FLAC, TrueHD and lossy are AAC, MP3, Opus; "Most anime uses ASS" and styling "breaks when there is incompatibility somewhere during playback"; fonts are "embedded in MKV as attachments or shipped in a separate folder" and the guide recommends muxing them in — [thewiki.moe Quality guide](https://thewiki.moe/guides/quality/).
- Release-type sizes: remux ~6 GB/episode, BD encode typically 1–2 GB, WEB-DL ~1.3 GB; WEB-DL groups listed include SubsPlease, Erai-raws, AnoZu, Yameii, ToonsHub, Kitsune, VARYG; fansub groups include Commie, GJM, Kaleido-subs, MTBB, Vodes etc.; Crunchyroll WEB is "2-pass 8 Mb/s or Majin VBR", Netflix 2-pass 5 Mb/s, HIDIVE 2-pass 5.3 Mb/s — [thewiki.moe Quality guide](https://thewiki.moe/guides/quality/).
- The same guide does not mention AV1, VP9, EAC3, PGS or ordered chapters at all; it does recommend choosing releases with LayoutRes set (otherwise typesetting shows broken perspective) and setting the subtitle colorspace matrix (BT.709 subs otherwise "will result in wrong colors") — [thewiki.moe Quality guide](https://thewiki.moe/guides/quality/).
- Wikipedia's "Pirated movie release types" page says "as of 2026, a few encoders use AV1" (movie-focused, not anime); AV2 was published 28 May 2026 — [Wikipedia: Pirated movie release types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirated_movie_release_types), [Wikipedia: AV2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AV2).
- Jellyfin remux definition: "When the container is unsupported, Jellyfin remuxes it. The video and audio streams are kept but rewrapped, which is the least CPU-intensive step"; MP4 "is one of the few containers that is never remuxed"; MKV "is remuxed on clients that don't support it"; subtitles the container cannot carry "are either converted to a supported format or burned into the video. Burning in is the most CPU-intensive option" — [Jellyfin Codec Support](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/clients/codec-support/).
- Jellyfin subtitle-by-container table: ASS/SSA, SRT, WebVTT, PGS and VobSub all supported in MKV; in TS only VobSub and EIA-608/708; in MP4 VobSub and MP4TT/TXTT (ASS/PGS not supported in MP4 or TS) — [Jellyfin Codec Support](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/clients/codec-support/).
- Jellyfin audio: FLAC, AAC, EAC3 and Opus show supported across Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari/Android/Android TV/iOS/Swiftfin/Roku/Kodi; DTS unsupported in all browsers and iOS (only DTS mono tested); EAC3 "Only EAC3 2.0 has been tested"; Opus on Safari only in .caf or stereo-in-MP4 on iOS 17/macOS 14+; TrueHD is not in the table at all — [Jellyfin Codec Support](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/clients/codec-support/).
- Plex MKV direct-play video codecs listed: H.264, HEVC, MPEG-4, MS-MPEG-4 v2/v3, VC-1, VP9, WMV3; MKV audio: AAC, AC-3, ALAC, E-AC-3, FLAC, MP2, MP3; "Our app does support audio passthrough of both DTS and TrueHD audio streams" but only with full Direct Play, not Direct Stream or transcode; "Other supported audio formats will be automatically transcoded for compatibility"; 4K direct play requires "No subtitles are enabled"; "Transcoding 4K content can be a CPU-intensive process" — [Plex: media formats supported (Xbox article)](https://support.plex.tv/articles/203824396-what-media-formats-are-supported/). Opus and AV1 are not listed on that page.
- Plex "Burn Subtitles — Automatic (Default)": "Burn in subtitles when the subtitle is an image-based format (e.g. VOBSUB, PGS, SUB/IDX, etc.) as well as for complex ASS/SSA subtitles" — [Plex Settings: Plex Mobile](https://support.plex.tv/articles/205671068-settings-plex-mobile/).
- Emby's subtitles support article lists external formats "ass, srt, ssa, sub/idx, and vtt" with no further format detail — [Emby Subtitles article](https://emby.media/support/articles/Subtitles.html).
- Jellyfin 12.0: "External subtitles can be embedded into MKV when transcoding", "Client-rendered graphical subtitles are allowed during remux", "Avoid SSA to ASS conversion and loss of styles", "The subtitle extraction timeout is now configurable"; 12.2: "Serve graphical subtitles as raw files with range support" — [jellyfin releases](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/releases).
- A third-party guide says subtitle configuration moved "from server-wide settings to per-library settings in Jellyfin 12" and recommends external SRT as safest; PGS "often force Jellyfin to burn subtitles into the video, which requires full video transcoding" — [smallgrid.uk](https://smallgrid.uk/guides/jellyfin-subtitles-causing-transcoding/) (unverified against official docs).

### Inferences
- For anime, the decision tree is dominated by two things: (a) whether the client can decode the video (HEVC Main10 is widely OK; Hi10P is not; see Q2) and (b) whether the client can render ASS itself. If (b) fails, every server falls to burn-in, which converts a would-be remux into a full video transcode.
- Because MP4/TS cannot carry ASS or PGS (Jellyfin table), any transcode to an HLS/TS or fMP4 target necessarily either extracts subs out-of-band (text) or burns them in (image or ASS when client-side rendering isn't available).
- AV1 anime releases exist from newer groups per the task premise, but I found no community source quantifying adoption; the primary quality guide still frames releases as HEVC/AVC.

### Gaps
- No authoritative 2026 statistic on the share of anime releases in AV1 or VP9/WebM; the thewiki.moe guide and Nyaa-group tables I could reach do not mention AV1.
- Plex's main "What media formats are supported" and the hardware-transcoding article returned HTTP 403/401 to fetchers, so Plex's Opus/AV1/10-bit official statements are only known from search snippets.
- No official per-client Plex or Emby codec matrix comparable to Jellyfin's table was retrievable.

## Key Question 2: Which codecs do current Android TV / Google TV / Apple TV / Shield devices decode in hardware, and which force transcoding (Hi10P)?

### Takeaway
H.264 High 10 (Hi10P) has no hardware decoder on Shield, Apple TV, Intel, NVIDIA or AMD; only Apple Silicon Macs and Rockchip SoCs are cited by Jellyfin as exceptions, so Hi10P is either CPU-decoded on the client or transcoded on the server. HEVC Main10 is broadly hardware-decoded; AV1 is hardware-decoded on Google TV Streamer-class devices and Android TV 10+ but not on the 2019 Shield or the A15-based Apple TV 4K.

### Cited Findings
- Jellyfin: "Hardware decoding of 10-bit H.264/AVC (High 10 profile) is not supported on Intel, NVIDIA, or AMD GPUs. It is only supported on Apple Silicon and Rockchip"; Jellyfin falls back to software decoding and the page recommends converting such files to HEVC 10-bit (Main 10) — [Jellyfin Hardware Acceleration](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/).
- Jellyfin NVIDIA page lists H.264 10-bit as "Blackwell and newer" but "(WIP, it's not currently enabled in Jellyfin)" — [Jellyfin NVIDIA HWA](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/nvidia/).
- NVIDIA staff on the GeForce forums: the Shield's hardware decoder has never supported Hi10P, though it does support 10-bit H.265 — [NVIDIA Shield forum](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/forums/shield-tv/9/271263/nvidia-shield-atvkodi10bit-mkv-playback/); users report Shield falls back to software decode for 10-bit H.264 with frame drops — [Kodi forum](https://forum.kodi.tv/showthread.php?tid=355127).
- Kodi wiki: no hardware decoders exist for Hi10P and none are planned; it always needs CPU decoding and many ARM processors are too weak — [Kodi Wiki Archive:Hi10P](https://kodi.wiki/view/Archive:Hi10P).
- Jellyfin client table (legend: "supported (hardware with software fallback)"): H.264 10-bit — Chrome/Edge/Android/Android TV/Swiftfin/Kodi/JMP supported, Firefox/iOS/Roku not, Safari conditional (must be enabled manually; requires macOS 14+ on Apple Silicon); HEVC 10-bit — Android TV and Roku "depends on device/settings", Safari/iOS/Swiftfin/Kodi/JMP supported; AV1 — Android TV conditional ("Requires Android TV 10 or later"), iOS not supported, Swiftfin conditional (on by default with VLCKit, off with native AVKit); VP9 — iOS not supported, Swiftfin software-only via VLCKit; Roku HEVC only on 4K devices — [Jellyfin Codec Support](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/clients/codec-support/).
- Shield AV1: software-only; "Shield TV supports software decoding of AV1, up to 1080p, through libdav1d once you install #vlc media player for Android TV" (2023) — [X post](https://x.com/gx_saurav/status/1728938190344724851). In early 2026 Nvidia said it will keep updating Shield software and is "exploring new hardware with AV1, HDR10+ and expanded Dolby Vision support" — [FlatpanelsHD](https://www.flatpanelshd.com/news.php?subaction=showfull&id=1770027899), [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/01/nvidia-shield-tv-update-support-new-hardware-rumors.html). A FlatpanelsHD headline (Oct 2026) reports a Shield TV Pro price hike as the base Shield TV is discontinued — conflicting with other sources; unverified.
- Apple TV 4K (2022, A15): no hardware AV1 decode; can decode AV1 in software with limitations, which is why Netflix HDR10+/AV1 streams don't show HDR — [FlatpanelsHD](https://www.flatpanelshd.com/news.php?subaction=showfull&id=1694598394), [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/739953). A17 Pro (iPhone 15 Pro) was Apple's first chip with a dedicated AV1 decoder — [HDTVTest](https://www.hdtvtest.co.uk/news/Apples-latest-iPhone-gets-hardware-accelerated-AV1-support). Apple's official spec page still lists the A15 Bionic — [Apple TV 4K specs](https://www.apple.com/apple-tv-4k/specs/); a new model with A17 Pro/A18/A19 is rumored (9to5Mac, Dec 31 2025) but unconfirmed — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/31/new-apple-tv-4k-is-coming-heres-what-the-rumors-say/).
- Google TV Streamer: supports AV1 whereas Chromecast 4K does not (third-party blog; hardware vs software not specified) — [Look Digital Signage blog](https://www.lookdigitalsignage.com/blog/google-tv-streamer). Google pushed AV1 support into Android TV 10 — [What Hi-Fi](https://www.whathifi.com/news/google-pushing-av1-support-into-android-tv-10).
- Jellyfin Android TV client does not render ASS natively: "ASS subtitle codec is not supported" in v0.19.0-beta.1 was closed as not planned — [jellyfin-androidtv #4922](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-androidtv/issues/4922); "Android TV official app can't play SSA/ASS subtitles" — [jellyfin #9964](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/9964); the Android client "relies on Media3's built-in ExoPlayer and does not handle ASS subtitles well", libass/mpv integration efforts "seem to have stalled" — [jellyfin-android #1833](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-android/issues/1833). The Android TV client has an "always burn in subtitles when transcoding" option — [Releasebot jellyfin-androidtv summary](https://releasebot.io/updates/jellyfin) (aggregator).
- Plex iOS default player renders ASS poorly under Automatic (Roku and web burn it in), with "Override Subtitles Styles" as the reported fix — [Plex forum](https://forums.plex.tv/t/automatic-subtitles-and-ass-ssa-fails-on-ios-default-player-vs-other-players-and-old-player/714181).

### Inferences
- On a Shield / Android TV box, a typical fansub release (HEVC Main10 + FLAC + ASS with fonts) will direct-play video and audio, but because the official Jellyfin Android TV client cannot render ASS, it will either show plain extracted text (losing typesetting) or trigger server burn-in; a Hi10P x264 release additionally forces video transcoding on any Intel/NVIDIA/AMD server (software decode step) and CPU decode on the client.
- Devices without AV1 hardware decode (2019 Shield, A15 Apple TV 4K) will force server-side AV1 decode -> HEVC/H.264 encode for AV1 anime releases, so AV1 decode capability on the server GPU (Intel Gen 12+, NVIDIA Ampere+) matters more than AV1 encode.

### Gaps
- Google TV Streamer official spec page was truncated by the fetcher; whether AV1 decode is hardware (MediaTek MT8696) is not confirmed from a primary source.
- Whether the 2026 Apple TV refresh shipped (rumored for an Oct 13, 2026 timing) and its codec list could not be confirmed.
- No per-device table for Fire TV / Roku Ultra AV1 or Hi10P was found beyond the Jellyfin client matrix.

## Key Question 3: Exactly what ffmpeg filter chain does Jellyfin use to burn in ASS, and what are the quality/performance implications with QSV/NVENC?

### Takeaway
Jellyfin renders ASS with the libass-backed `subtitles=` filter on the CPU — in the software path it is inline in `-vf` after `format=yuv420p`; in the hardware (QSV) path it draws onto a transparent `alphasrc` canvas, `hwupload`s that layer, and composites with `overlay_qsv` so the video frames never leave the GPU. The CPU cost of libass rasterization remains in both paths, and the font attachments are supplied via `fontsdir=` pointing at Jellyfin's extracted-attachments cache.

### Cited Findings
- Jellyfin lists subtitle burn-in as an optional pipeline stage ("Decoding, deinterlacing (optional), scaling and format conversion (optional), HDR/DV tone-mapping (optional), subtitle burn-in (optional), encoding, and zero-copy across these stages") and warns "Some of these stages cannot be GPU accelerated due to software, hardware or driver limitations", with partial acceleration meaning higher CPU use — [Jellyfin Hardware Acceleration](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/).
- Actual QSV burn-in command (Jellyfin web 10.10.1, Debian 12, Intel G4600, iHD 24.4.1): subtitle branch `alphasrc=s=1920x1080:r=23.976025:start='0',format=bgra,subtitles=f=...:alpha=1:sub2video=1:fontsdir=...,hwupload=derive_device=qsv:extra_hw_frames=64[sub]`; composite `[main][sub]overlay_qsv=eof_action=pass:repeatlast=0:w=3840:h=2160`; device init `-init_hw_device vaapi=va:,vendor_id=0x8086,driver=iHD -init_hw_device qsv=qs@va`; encoder `-codec:v:0 hevc_qsv -tag:v:0 hvc1 -preset veryfast`. Software path: `-vf ... format=yuv420p,subtitles=f=...` then `-codec:v:0 libx265 -tag:v:0 hvc1 -preset veryfast -crf 28` — [Jellyfin forum: burn-in subtitles semi-transparent with HWA](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-the-burn-in-subtitles-will-become-semi-transparent-when-enable-hardware-acceleration).
- Software burn-in command fragments from bug reports: `subtitles=f='/config/data/subtitles/f/<id>.ass'` with `fontsdir='/cache/attachments/<media-id>'` (10.10.7, FFmpeg 7.0.2-Jellyfin) — [jellyfin #13914](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/13914); on Windows `subtitles=f='C\:/Users/.../data/subtitles/5a/<id>/1.ass':fontsdir='C\:/Users/.../data/attachments/5a/<id>'` with `libx264` and a `scale`/`format` chain, no alpha/hwupload/overlay (10.11.6, jellyfin-ffmpeg 7.1.3) — [jellyfin #16334](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/16334).
- Upstream `subtitles` filter options: `fontsdir` adds a directory of fonts alongside the system font provider (added Sept 2015); `original_size` is needed because "a misdesign in ASS aspect ratio arithmetic" makes it necessary "to correctly scale the fonts if the aspect ratio has been changed"; `force_style` overrides styles with comma-separated ASS KEY=VALUE pairs; `alpha` enables alpha-channel processing; also `filename/f`, `charenc`, `stream_index/si` — [FFmpeg cvslog: allow setting fonts directory](https://ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-cvslog/2015-September/093197.html), [MLT avfilter.subtitles reference](https://www.mltframework.org/plugins/FilterAvfilter-subtitles/).
- The standard `overlay` filter is software-only: decoded frames are downloaded to system memory, overlaid on the CPU, then uploaded back for encoding (FFmpeg-user reply, Oct 2017) — [ffmpeg-user list](https://ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-user/2017-October/037602.html).
- Jellyfin "Allow subtitle extraction on the fly" delivers embedded subtitles as text to avoid video transcoding; "On some systems, this can take a long time and cause video playback to stall during the extraction process"; disabling it makes embedded subs burn in when the client lacks native support; a moderator says burn-in "is the most intensive method of transcoding, because two transcodings happen at once, applying the subtitle layer on top of the video layer"; moderators recommend the Subtitle Extract plugin to pre-extract — [Jellyfin forum threads via search](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-question-about-subtitles-extraction), [forum: subtitle issues](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-solved-subtitle-issues?page=2).
- Fallback fonts: Dashboard > Playback has "Fallback font folder path" and "Enable fallback fonts" (used by some clients to render subtitles); a user reported that with fallback fonts the ASS-specified font was ignored and a random font from the directory chosen — [Jellyfin forum: Changing subtitle font](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-changing-subtitle-font).
- Hardware-specific caveats: Intel QSV VPP tone-mapping is "currently only available on Linux"; OpenCL tone-mapping supports DV P5 and needs the OpenCL runtime (N95/N100 and Arc A380 need runtime 23.xx or newer); Intel Compute-Runtime 25.18.33578.6+ is broken on Arc A-series (use 25.13.33276.16); Arc B-series requires Resizable BAR or the media driver crashes; NVIDIA: "On Windows and Linux CUDA is the only available tone-mapping method"; NVENC session caps by driver: 3 (<530), 5 (530–546), 8 (550–58x), 12 (590+), with an unofficial patch to bypass — [Jellyfin Intel HWA](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/intel/), [Jellyfin Known Issues](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/known-issues/), [Jellyfin NVIDIA HWA](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/nvidia/).
- nyanmisaka's Jellyfin build docs list subtitle burn-in as supported on Intel QSV and on the Vulkan/VPP paths — [nyanmisaka/jellyfin on Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/nyanmisaka/jellyfin).
- 10.11.0 changelog items (aggregator summary): offloading 1080p+ subtitle scaling to RKRGA, and a fix for ASS subtitle transparency in hardware transcoding — [Releasebot Jellyfin](https://releasebot.io/updates/jellyfin). jellyfin-ffmpeg 8.1.2-3: "Backport a fix to not stall sub2video on stream EOF"; 8.1.2-2: "Add D3D11 overlay/tonemap/transpose/deint/scale filters for Qualcomm"; 8.1.1-2: "Fix extracting mkv attachments with non-ascii filenames"; 8.1.1-3 / 7.1.4-3: "Relax to allow safe filenames in mkv attachments" — [jellyfin-ffmpeg releases](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-ffmpeg/releases).
- Raspberry Pi 5 burn-in anecdote: CPU went from 5–30% to near 100% when PGS subs were burned in; the log showed CPU overlay plus software x264 — [Jellyfin forum: performance issue with subtitles](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-performance-issue-with-subtitles).

### Inferences
- In the HW path only the compositing (overlay_qsv / overlay_cuda / overlay_vaapi / overlay_opencl) and the scaling of the video are on the GPU; libass still rasterizes each ASS frame on the CPU at the output canvas size (`alphasrc=s=WxH`), then that BGRA layer is uploaded. For heavy typesetting (karaoke, moving signs) the per-frame libass cost scales with output resolution and sign complexity, so multiple simultaneous burn-in streams are CPU-bound even on a strong GPU. This is consistent with the forum moderator's "two transcodings at once" description and the Pi 5 report, but I found no benchmark quantifying ms/frame.
- The `sub2video=1` option seen in Jellyfin's command does not appear in the upstream option list I could retrieve, suggesting it is a jellyfin-ffmpeg patch; jellyfin-ffmpeg's "stall sub2video on stream EOF" fix supports that it is maintained downstream (inference; unverified).
- Because the QSV canvas is created at the output size (e.g. 1920x1080 branch overlaid at w=3840:h=2160 in the example), resolution changes during transcoding can produce scaling of the rendered layer rather than re-rendering at native size, which is the plausible mechanism behind "mis-sized signs" reports (inference; see Q5 gaps).

### Gaps
- The Jellyfin subtitles documentation page returned empty content from the fetcher in three attempts (site and GitHub source path), so the official wording of "Burn subtitles" / "Always burn in subtitle when transcoding" options is cited only from forum/issue sources.
- No official Jellyfin documentation of the exact NVENC (CUDA) burn-in chain was retrievable; only the QSV example and software examples were found. The FFmpeg filters HTML page could not be read at the `subtitles` section because of fetch truncation, and the FFmpeg trac wiki on burning subtitles was behind Anubis bot protection.
- No quantitative benchmark of libass CPU cost per 1080p/4K burn-in stream in 2025–2026.

## Key Question 4: How do Plex and Emby handle ASS when the client can't render it, and do they preserve styling in burn-in?

### Takeaway
Both Plex and Emby burn in ASS via their transcoders when the client can't render it; Plex's default "Automatic" explicitly burns "complex ASS/SSA" and there is no server-wide override, while Emby staff have confirmed that burned-in fonts can differ from the embedded ones and offered no fix. Neither vendor documents a guarantee that embedded fonts/styling are preserved during burn-in.

### Cited Findings
- Plex Burn Subtitles options: "Automatic – (Default) Burn in subtitles when the subtitle is an image-based format (e.g. VOBSUB, PGS, SUB/IDX, etc.) as well as for complex ASS/SSA subtitles"; "Only image formats – Only burn in subtitles when the subtitle is an image-based format"; "Always – When used, subtitles will always be burned into the video, regardless of the original source type of the subtitle" — [Plex Settings: Plex Mobile](https://support.plex.tv/articles/205671068-settings-plex-mobile/), [Plex Web App Settings](https://support.plex.tv/articles/200289408-plex-web-app-settings/), [Plex Settings: Android TV](https://support.plex.tv/articles/settings-android-tv/).
- There is no way to set "Only image formats" as a server-side default; each client must set it — [Plex forum](https://forums.plex.tv/t/subtitles-only-image-formats-as-server-default/163393). On Android, "Automatic will burn them [ASS]. Changing the setting to Only Image Formats will now natively play them" (2018 user report) — [Plex forum feature request](https://forums.plex.tv/t/new-option-burn-subtitles-avoid-at-all-costs/320968).
- Plex forum users state burned-in subtitles are re-transcoded on every play (no cached copy) and that the Burn Subtitles setting "will not reduce transcoding much"; the thread contains no Plex staff replies — [Plex forum: Subtitles transcoded (burned) into the video stream](https://forums.plex.tv/t/subtitles-transcoded-burned-into-the-video-stream/576709).
- Plex burn-in bug (2026): 10-bit HEVC hardware transcode with ASS burn-in on AMD VAAPI loses all chroma and plays grayscale; disabling HW encoding or subtitles resolves it — [Plex forum bug report](https://forums.plex.tv/t/bug-video-renders-black-and-white-chroma-lost-when-burning-in-ass-subtitles-with-amd-vaapi-hardw/940505).

## Key Question 5: Known Jellyfin burn-in issues for anime (fonts, sizing, delays, sync, ordered chapters)

### Takeaway
Documented Jellyfin burn-in problems include font fallback when attachments aren't extracted (Android path), wrong fonts in browser rendering, semi-transparent ASS under QSV, burn-in silently not applied in 10.11.0, long stalls on large files, subtitle desync during transcodes, VAAPI corruption/stutter, and memory allocation failures; several were closed as stale rather than fixed.

### Cited Findings
- Fonts fall back to default on Android because `AttachmentExtractor` doesn't run when playback starts from the Android app: "The attachment folder stays empty," so ffmpeg has no fonts for `fontsdir`; workaround is to play once from the web client; affected 10.10.0+ (reported 10.10.7, FFmpeg 7.0.2-Jellyfin); a maintainer pointed to the unstable branch rewrite; closed as "not planned" Sep 5, 2025 due to inactivity — [jellyfin #13914](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/13914).
- Embedded TTF always used regardless of ASS style (10.11.6, jellyfin-ffmpeg 7.1.3, Android 2.6.3, Chromium 145/Firefox 148): browser console shows libass (subtitles-octopus) selecting the fallback font; unaffected in the Windows desktop app, Roku, mpv, VLC; closed as not planned Aug 2026 — [jellyfin #16334](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/16334).
- Burn-in no longer applied on 10.11.0 (Firefox/Linux): the PlaybackInfo request's SubtitleProfiles array stops the server adding `SubtitleMethod=Encode` to the TranscodingUrl; reporter suggested changing BurnSubtitles from "auto" to "all" in `browserDeviceProfile.js`; closed as completed Oct 25, 2025 with no linked PR — [jellyfin-web #7254](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-web/issues/7254).
- Semi-transparent ASS burn-in with Intel QSV (Debian 12, G4600, iHD 24.4.1, web 10.10.1): only subtitles with transparency affected; nyanmisaka requested files; "Fate could not reproduce"; no confirmed fix in-thread — [Jellyfin forum](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-the-burn-in-subtitles-will-become-semi-transparent-when-enable-hardware-acceleration). A 10.11.0 changelog item is described as a fix for ASS subtitle transparency in hardware transcoding — [Releasebot](https://releasebot.io/updates/jellyfin) (aggregator).
- Playback stalls before transcoding starts on large files needing burn-in, even with Quick Sync — [jellyfin #11938](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/11938).
- "Cannot allocate memory" crash during burn-in on LiveTV, fixed by raising extra hardware frames from 24 to 64 — [jellyfin #11052](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/11052) (consistent with `extra_hw_frames=64` in the current QSV chain).
- Subtitle out of sync while transcoding on Android TV; reporter says it goes away on web with "Always burn in subtitle when transcoding", an option the Android TV app lacked — [jellyfin-androidtv #4399](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-androidtv/issues/4399); SRT sync off only when video/audio is transcoded (10.9.2, M2 Mac mini, VideoToolbox) — [jellyfin #11825](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/11825); Roku: desynced external subs stay unusable until restart, requests automatic burn-in when transcoding — [jellyfin-roku #347](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-roku/issues/347).
- Burn-in stutter/corruption on AMD iGPU with VAAPI — [jellyfin #9601](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/9601); DVBSUB burned at wrong position with the QSV overlay path while software overlay is correct — [jellyfin #9670](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/9670); older VAAPI burn-in filter-order failure where a reordered chain gave ~2.5x speedup — [jellyfin #2238](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/2238).
- Unnecessary burn-in: converting ASS to SRT would avoid a full video transcode — [jellyfin #2203](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/2203); transcoding triggered only by an unsupported subtitle codec — [jellyfin #7809](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/7809).
- Extraction of MKS attachments fails (tested with ffmpeg 7.1.2) — [jellyfin #16303](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/16303); embedded ASS plus fonts with Windows paths made ffmpeg fail — [jellyfin #11692](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/11692); "Subtitles will stop ffmpeg with error 38" — [jellyfin-ffmpeg #630](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-ffmpeg/issues/630).
- Missing characters in burned-in subs: a moderator said ensure the required fonts are available on the server machine; embedding the font as an attachment is the common fix — [Jellyfin forum: Subtitles Problem](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-subtitles-problem).
- Known HW issues affecting tone-mapped anime transcodes: Intel Windows driver bug (green/blue screen with tone-mapping, fixed in 31.0.101.5590); Linux kernels 5.18–6.1.3 lock up i915 during OpenCL tone-mapping; AMD Adrenalin 24.1.1–24.4.1 error -60 on RDNA; some distros (Fedora, openSUSE, Manjaro) disable H.264/HEVC in Mesa VA-API — [Jellyfin Known Issues](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/known-issues/).

### Inferences
- The two font bugs (#13914, #16334) show that font correctness depends on a separate attachment-extraction step keyed by media ID; any client path that skips it yields libass fallback fonts. Ensuring fonts are pre-extracted (play from web once, or use the Subtitle Extract plugin) is the practical mitigation.
- Several "out of sync" reports concern extracted text subtitles delivered out-of-band during transcodes rather than burn-in itself; burn-in is paradoxically the workaround for sync but the most expensive.

### Gaps
- I found no GitHub issue specifically documenting mis-sized signs when transcoding changes resolution (the `original_size` scaling concern); this remains plausible from the filter semantics but is unsourced.
- Whether the 10.11.0 burn-in regression (#7254) was fixed in 10.11.x or 12.x could not be determined (closed "completed" with no linked PR).

## Key Question 6: Does any server support MKV ordered chapters / segment linking?

### Takeaway
No — Plex, Jellyfin and (by absence of any evidence) Emby do not support ordered chapters or segment linking; the limitation originates in FFmpeg itself (an open ticket for over a decade), while mpv supports ordered chapters natively and VLC only partially.

### Cited Findings
- Plex: "chapters are supported, ordered chapters (custom orders, and also segment linking to external files) is not"; multiple feature requests (2017: "a lot of classic anime is available only in this format"; 2018; a 2024 "True Ordered Chapters (NOT segment linking)" request); episodes play without OP/ED when those are in linked files — [Plex forum: MKV Ordered Chapters Support](https://forums.plex.tv/t/mkv-ordered-chapters-support/3839?page=2), [Plex feature request: Matroska Linked Segments / Ordered Chapters](https://forums.plex.tv/t/feature-request-matroska-linked-segments-ordered-chapters/202377), [Plex: True Ordered Chapters](https://forums.plex.tv/t/true-ordered-chapters-not-segment-linking-in-mkv/540146).
- Jellyfin: 2019 enhancement request "Obey mkv chapter lists in web player" remains unimplemented — [jellyfin #980](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/980), [Jellyfin feature request #104](https://features.jellyfin.org/posts/104/obey-mkv-chapter-lists-in-web-player-by-default-or-optionally); the downstream Jellyfin request "support mkv ordered chapters / segment linking" is open on its feature board — [features.jellyfin.org/posts/390](https://features.jellyfin.org/posts/390/support-mkv-ordered-chapters-segment-linking).
- FFmpeg ticket #3123 (ordered chapters) was "Opened 10 years ago" and is still open — [FFmpeg trac #3123](https://fftrac-bg.ffmpeg.org/ticket/3123).
- mpv supports ordered chapters natively but not hard segment linking; a community script adds limited support using mkvinfo — [mpv-segment-linking](https://github.com/CogentRedTester/mpv-segment-linking). VLC: "doesn't play previous and next segments if the mkvs have chapters" — [VLC trac #1222](https://trac.videolan.org/vlc/ticket/1222). Kodi/OSMC: used in anime where one OP/ED file is shared; work was ongoing — [OSMC forum](https://discourse.osmc.tv/t/mkv-segment-linking-ordered-chapters/70190).
- Jellyfin chapter-related bugs for anime: chapters outside runtime cause Chapter Segments Provider range errors — [jellyfin #15059](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/15059); intro/outro inference from "Opening"/"Ending" chapter names is an open request — [jellyfin #16663](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/issues/16663).

### Inferences
- Because all three servers transcode and probe via FFmpeg, server-side support would require FFmpeg demuxer support first; until then, releases using segment linking must be remuxed into self-contained episodes (e.g. with MKVToolNix) for any media server.

### Gaps
- No Emby-specific statement (forum or docs) on ordered chapters was found; the absence is inferred.

## Key Question 7: What hardware is recommended in 2026 for an anime server that must burn in subs for multiple streams?

### Takeaway
Jellyfin's own guidance says a GPU is always recommended (even a basic Intel iGPU rivals a Ryzen 5800X for transcoding) and ranks encoder quality NVIDIA RTX 50 ≥ Intel Arc-B ≥ Apple ≥ Intel ≥ NVIDIA ≥ AMD; community consensus favors Intel Arc A310/A380 as the cheap dedicated transcoder, but since libass rendering is CPU-bound, burn-in-heavy anime servers need CPU headroom in addition to the GPU — N100-class boxes are rated at only 3–4 concurrent 4K HEVC transcodes before subtitle load.

### Cited Findings
- Jellyfin Hardware Selection: CPU-only "is not recommended, and HDR-to-SDR tone-mapping makes it worse"; "a GPU is always recommended, where even a basic Intel iGPU can handle as much load as a Ryzen 5800X for this use case"; encoder quality ordering "NVIDIA (RTX 50) ≥ Intel (Arc-B) ≥ Apple ≥ Intel ≥ NVIDIA ≥ AMD ≥ AMD H.264 (RX 9000)" with AMD least preferred because its H.264 encoders were weak before RDNA4; avoid "F" suffix CPUs (no iGPU); RX 6400/6500 lack encoders; baseline "100GB SSD for your OS, Jellyfin files and transcoding cache" and "Gigabit Ethernet Adapter or faster, WiFi or Powerline not recommended" — [Jellyfin Hardware Selection](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/administration/hardware-selection/).
- Jellyfin Intel tiers: Entry (HD/UHD 600/605/61x), Mainstream (HD/UHD 620/630, Iris 640/655, Gen 11), High-Performance (UHD 7xx, Iris Xe), "Hardcore" (Arc A and B-series); HEVC 10-bit decode from Kaby Lake / Apollo Lake / Gemini Lake; AV1 decode Tiger Lake+; AV1 encode Arc A-series / Meteor Lake+; Arc A-series and Jasper/Elkhart Lake support only low-power (HuC) encode mode; "GuC & HuC firmware can be missing on older distros"; Arc B-series needs kernel 6.12+ and mandatory ReBAR; ASPM recommended for idle power — [Jellyfin Intel HWA](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/intel/).
- Arc A310 in practice: ZimaOS lists the A310 in its Intel compatibility table and says to verify with intel_gpu_top under a forced transcode — [ZimaOS A310 vs RX 6400](https://shop.zimaspace.com/pages/jellyfin-zimaos-gpu-intel-arc-a310-rx6400); a 2024 Jellyfin forum thread: A310 detected but "due to the lack of ReBAR support, the speed will be greatly reduced in some scenarios" — [Jellyfin forum: A310 transcoding help](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-solved-a310-transcoding-help).
- JellyWatch 2026 comparison (blog, undated; related posts June 2026): N100 iGPU 3–4 concurrent 4K HEVC transcodes, no AV1 encode, 10 W; Arc A380 6–8, AV1 encode, 75 W, ~$100; Arc A770 10–12; GTX 1650 3–4; RTX 3060 8–10; RTX 4060 10–12 with AV1, 115 W; RX 6600 4–6; RX 7600 6–8; recommends N100 for 1–4 users, Arc A380 for 5–8, RTX 4060 for 10+; notes Arc Linux drivers "still less mature than NVIDIA"; claims Direct Play uses no GPU and that "RTX cards have no limit" on NVENC sessions (contradicted by Jellyfin's Known Issues page, which says consumer GeForce cards are capped by driver version: 3/5/8/12) — [JellyWatch blog](https://jellywatch.app/blog/best-gpu-for-jellyfin-transcoding-2026-intel-nvidia-amd); contradicted by [Jellyfin Known Issues](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/known-issues/).
- NVIDIA minimums for Jellyfin 10.11: driver 522.25 (Windows) / 520.56.06 (Linux); HEVC 10-bit decode from Maxwell GM206, encode from Pascal; AV1 decode Ampere, encode Ada (needs Jellyfin 10.9+); DV tone-mapping needs "Enable enhanced NVDEC decoder" — [Jellyfin NVIDIA HWA](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/nvidia/).
- Jellyfin forum consensus: Intel Arc (A380/A310) is the go-to cheap dedicated transcoder; 4K HDR multi-stream usually needs a dGPU rather than an iGPU; tone-mapping "can be memory-hungry on iGPUs with limited assignable VRAM" — [Jellyfin forum: GPU recommendation](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-gpu-recommendation-for-transcoding), [Jellyfin forum: hardware for 4-5 4K](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-hardware-for-4-5-4k-and-home-lab). Jellyfin docs add that iGPUs share system memory so dual-channel RAM helps tone-mapping, and an SSD/RamDisk for transcode segments helps — [Jellyfin Hardware Acceleration](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/).
- N100 + first-gen Fire TV transcoding problems thread exists (content not fetched) — [Jellyfin forum](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-transcoding-issues-with-intel-n100-cpu-and-first-generation-fire-tv-client).
- Plex: NAS users "will require JasperLake or better CPU to reliably transcode to HEVC output"; HW transcoding needs Plex Pass; AMD unsupported for HEVC encode — [Plex: Using Hardware-Accelerated Streaming](https://support.plex.tv/articles/115002178853-using-hardware-accelerated-streaming/) (via snippet), [Plex: Is a NAS right for me](https://support.plex.tv/articles/201373793-is-plex-media-server-on-a-nas-right-for-me/).
- Apple Silicon and Rockchip are the only platforms Jellyfin lists with Hi10P hardware decode; Rockchip RK3588 gets DV tone-mapping in 10.11+ — [Jellyfin Hardware Acceleration](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/).

### Inferences
- For an anime library with many Hi10P x264 files, an Apple Silicon Mac mini (VideoToolbox) or RK3588 board is the only option that hardware-decodes them; on Intel/NVIDIA/AMD the decode step is CPU software, so the CPU, not the GPU, bounds Hi10P stream count. Converting Hi10P to HEVC Main10 (Jellyfin's recommendation) removes this.
- Since burn-in adds a CPU-side libass rasterization per stream, an N100 (4 E-cores) is likely to run out of CPU before its iGPU runs out of encode capacity when several ASS burn-ins run concurrently; an N305 (8 E-cores) or a desktop Core with UHD 7xx, or a cheap Arc A310/A380 paired with a stronger CPU, is a more defensible choice. This is an inference — no source benchmarks burn-in stream counts per CPU.
- Used Quadro cards remain attractive mainly for unlimited NVENC sessions and CUDA tone-mapping, but lack AV1 decode (pre-Ampere), which matters as AV1 anime releases grow.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 measurement of simultaneous ASS burn-in streams on N100/N305/A310/Quadro hardware was found; the only burn-in load data point is the Pi 5 PGS anecdote.
- The jellyfin.org "Hardware Selection" page content was obtained via search snippets rather than full fetch; the exact "tiers" of Intel iGPU quality and any model-specific burn-in notes may be incomplete.
- Reddit r/jellyfin / r/PleX 2025–2026 hardware threads were not directly retrievable in this session.
